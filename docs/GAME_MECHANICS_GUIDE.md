# Chaos Creatures: Complete Player Guide

## Welcome to Chaos Creatures

Chaos Creatures is a collectible card game where every card you own is genuinely unique. The art, flavor text, and powers on your cards were generated by AI, and they evolve visually and mechanically through play. No two players have the same collection. No two Legendaries look the same. The card you build over 100 games is yours alone.

The game's central tension is the conflict between Order and Chaos — and every turn, a roll of the dice decides which side of that conflict you're fighting on.

---

## Part 1: The Factions

Your first and most important decision is choosing your faction. Everything flows from this choice: your deck's card pool, your playstyle, and the unique mechanical identity that separates your strategy from every other player's.

**There are five factions. Decks must be built from a single faction — no mixing.**

### The Ironwright Collective
*Brutalist space-industrial empire. Concrete, rebar, void-forged iron, orbital machinery.*

**Exclusive Mechanic: Augment** — Augment modifiers stack on top of each other on a single creature, compounding in power as more are added. The Ironwright identity is tall, not wide: invest heavily in a small number of creatures, each becoming a fortress of compounding effects.

Sub-factions: **The Foundry Directorate** (Order-aligned) · **The Scrap Legions** (Chaos-aligned)

### The Fey Courts
*Ancient forests, bioluminescent flora, antlered fey lords, mycelial networks.*

**Exclusive Mechanic: Bond** — Bond modifiers create synergies between your creatures, caring about how many others are on the board and what keywords they carry. Individually below the curve; a full board is overwhelming. Weakness: board wipes shrink your network and weaken every Bond simultaneously.

Sub-factions: **The Verdant Throne** (Order-aligned) · **The Hollow Court** (Chaos-aligned)

### The Demonic Kingdoms
*Hellfire, obsidian fortresses, blood rituals, corrupted flesh, infernal bargains.*

**Exclusive Mechanic: Corruption** — Corruption modifiers make dark bargains: take self-damage each turn in exchange for massive ATK gains, death triggers, or explosive effects. Demonic decks are on a clock — if the opponent stabilizes, your creatures eventually burn themselves out.

Sub-factions: **The Furnace Lords** (Chaos-aligned) · **The Obsidian Bureaucracy** (Order-aligned)

### The Celestial Crusade
*Self-righteous divine crusaders, angelic formations, holy gold armor, biblically accurate celestials.*

**Exclusive Mechanic: Exalt** — Exalt modifiers create aura effects that benefit ALL your creatures when specific board conditions are met (usually creature count thresholds). Build wide, maintain your formation, and watch every creature benefit from every aura simultaneously. Weakness: Exalt collapses when creature count drops below the threshold.

Sub-factions: **Knights of Deliverance** (Order-aligned) · **Heaven's Chosen** (Chaos-aligned)

### The Endless
*The undead — raised, summoned, abandoned. Necromancers, liches, spectres, bone constructs.*

**Exclusive Mechanic: Persist** — Persist modifiers trigger effects when your creatures die. Every kill is pyrrhic for your opponent. At higher tiers, some Persist effects trigger whenever ANY friendly creature dies. Weakness: all the value is back-loaded — if the game ends before your creatures die, you get nothing.

Sub-factions: **Necromantic Cabals** (Order-aligned) · **The Lost Spectres** (Chaos-aligned)

## Part 2: Building Your Deck

### Deck Rules

- **30 cards** per deck
- **Maximum 2 copies** of any card
- **Legendaries:** Max 2 per deck, max 1 copy each
- **Planar Ruins:** Max 2 per deck, max 1 on the field at a time
- **All cards must be from the same faction.** Neutral (unevolved) Planar Ruins can go in any deck

### Card Types

**Creatures** — Your primary fighting force. They have ATK, HP, a chaos mote cost, and an instability value.

**Spells** — One-shot effects played during your main phase. No instant-speed spells — all spells are played during your turn, before combat.

**Stabilizers** — Sit in the stability zone (separate from the 5 creature board slots). Free to play (0 motes), 1 per turn. Each has an activated ability you tap to use, then it goes on cooldown until next turn. Cannot be targeted by creature attacks.

**Planar Ruins** — Ancient structures that take a creature slot but provide powerful passive benefits. High HP, zero ATK. Opponents can direct creature attacks at your Ruins to destroy them — when destroyed, a negative penalty fires on your side.

### Choosing Your Avatar

When you build a deck, you pair it with an avatar from the same faction. Your avatar provides a base instability modifier that is always active — your deck's instability floor before any creatures hit the board.

## Part 3: How a Game Is Played

### Setup

Both players are assigned randomly — Player 1 and Player 2. To compensate for going second:

- **Player 1 draws 4 cards.** Player 2 draws 5 cards.
- **Player 2 receives a Chaos Spark** — a zero-cost spell that grants +1 chaos mote for one turn.
- Each player may take one free mulligan.
- Both players start at 0 chaos motes and 20 HP.
- **Player 1 cannot attack on turn 1.**

### Resources: Chaos Motes

Every turn, you gain 1 chaos mote (up to a maximum of 10). Motes carry over between turns — you don't lose unspent motes. There are no land or resource cards; your entire 30-card deck is creatures, spells, stabilizers, and Ruins.

### The Turn Structure

**1. Start of Turn** — All start-of-turn effects fire automatically (Corruption self-damage, aura recalculation, Ruin effects, etc.).

**2. The Chaos Roll** — A D20 rolls. The result is compared to your current instability:
- Roll **under** your instability → **Chaos Event** triggers
- Roll **over** your instability → **Order Event** triggers
- Roll **equal to** your instability → nothing happens

**3. Event Resolution** — The triggered event is applied; cards with matching triggered abilities then fire.

**4. Draw and Gain Mana** — Draw 1 card; gain 1 chaos mote (up to the cap of 10). No penalty for an empty deck.

**5. Main Phase** — Play creatures, spells, and Ruins from hand (mana cost applies). Play up to 1 stabilizer from hand into the stability zone (free). Activate ready stabilizers in your stability zone. Any order (60-second timer).

**6. Declare Attackers** — Select which creatures will attack.

**7. Assign Blockers** — Control passes to the defending player for 60 seconds. Assign one blocker per attacker.

**8. Combat Resolution** — All damage resolves simultaneously.

**9. End of Turn** — Temporary buffs expire; turn passes.

### The 60-Second Timer

At 15 seconds remaining, the timer bar turns red. At 0 seconds: if in the main phase, no attacks happen; if assigning blockers, all attackers hit face.

## Part 4: The Chaos Roll in Depth

### Instability: Your Chaos Rating

**Your instability = your avatar's base modifier + the instability values of all your creatures currently on the board.**

Each creature has an instability value (0–5 base), modified by evolution outcomes and certain modifiers. Instability is clamped between 1 and 20.

| Instability | Chance of Chaos Event | Chance of Order Event |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0% | 95% |
| 5 | 20% | 75% |
| 10 | 45% | 50% |
| 15 | 70% | 25% |
| 20 | 95% | 0% |

There is always a 5% chance of nothing (exact match).

**Order decks** aim for instability 3–6. **Chaos decks** aim for instability 14–18. **Hybrid decks** sit around 8–12.

### The Natural Comeback Mechanic

When you're losing — fewer creatures on board — your instability naturally drops, giving you more Order events: stabilizing buffs, healing, extra cards. When you're winning with a full evolved board, Chaos events can occasionally bite you. The player who's winning is always sitting on a live grenade.

### Order Events

| Event | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Mending Light** | Heal your most-damaged creature for 3 HP |
| **Planar Ward** | Grant Shield to a friendly creature of your choice |
| **Steady Growth** | All your creatures get +0/+1 HP permanently |
| **Clarity** | Draw 1 card |
| **Fortify** | A friendly creature of your choice gets +1/+1 permanently |
| **Sanctuary** | Heal your avatar for 3 HP |
| **Bulwark** | Your lowest-HP creature gets +0/+2 HP permanently |
| **Harmonize** | All your creatures heal 2 HP (full HP creatures get +0/+1 instead) |

### Chaos Events

| Event | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Surge** | A random friendly creature gets +3 ATK this turn |
| **Wildfire** | Deal 2 damage to a random enemy creature |
| **Upheaval** | Deal 1 damage to ALL creatures on both sides |
| **Frenzy** | ALL your creatures get +1 ATK this turn |
| **Rift Bolt** | Deal 3 damage directly to the enemy avatar |
| **Chaos Siphon** | Deal 2 damage to a random friendly creature; it gets +3 ATK permanently if it survives |
| **Maelstrom** | Deal 3 damage to a random creature on the board — either side |
| **Overcharge** | A random friendly creature gains +2 ATK and Piercing this turn |

Three of the eight Chaos events can hurt you: **Upheaval**, **Chaos Siphon**, and **Maelstrom**. This is the price of Chaos power.

## Part 5: Combat

### How Attacking Works

During the Declare Attackers phase, tap each creature you want to attack with. You can direct attackers at the opponent's HP or at the opponent's Planar Ruin if they have one on the field. Cast your buffs before committing to combat — you cannot play additional spells after confirming your attackers.

### Taunt: The Forced Engagement Rule

Taunt works in two parts:

1. **Forced Attack:** While you control a Taunt creature, your opponent must declare at least 1 attacker per Taunt creature you control.
2. **Forced Block:** Your Taunt creatures must be assigned as blockers if there is any attacker they can legally block.

Taunt's forced-block obligation is waived if all attacking creatures have Flying and your Taunt creature lacks Reach or Flying.

### Combat Damage Is Simultaneous

All damage resolves at the same instant. Both the attacker and the blocker deal their damage at the same time.

**Resolution sequence (per blocked combat pair):**

1. **Shield check:** If either creature has Shield, it absorbs ALL damage from that single source. Shield breaks. The creature takes 0 damage.
2. **Damage is dealt simultaneously** by both attacker and defender.
3. **Deathtouch:** If a creature with Deathtouch dealt any damage, the other creature is destroyed regardless of HP.
4. **Normal death check:** Creatures at 0 or below HP are destroyed.
5. **Piercing:** If the attacker has Piercing and dealt more damage than the blocker's HP, excess carries through to the defending player's HP. Piercing does not apply to blockers.
6. **Lifesteal:** The creature's controller heals for the amount of damage actually dealt (0 if Shield absorbed).

Unblocked attackers deal their ATK directly to the defending player's HP; Lifesteal applies to face damage as well.

After combat, destroyed creatures are removed, on-death effects fire (Persist triggers), and both players' instability values are recalculated.

## Part 6: Keywords

There are exactly 9 keywords. Keeping the set small means each keyword feels meaningful, and modifier-granted keywords are genuinely exciting.

| Keyword | Rules Text |
|---|---|
| **Shield** | Absorbs the first instance of damage, then breaks. Blocks ALL damage from one source — doesn't reduce it, just breaks. |
| **Lifesteal** | When this creature deals damage, your HP heals for the same amount. |
| **Flying** | Can only be blocked by Flying or Reach creatures. |
| **Reach** | Can block Flying creatures. No other effect. |
| **Deathtouch** | Any damage this creature deals to another creature destroys it, regardless of HP. |
| **Taunt** | Opponents must attack into this creature; this creature must block if it legally can. |
| **Piercing** | Excess combat damage to a blocker carries through to the opponent's HP. |
| **Haste** | When played, immediately attack a specific enemy creature as a bonus attack. Can also attack normally during the regular combat phase — effectively attacking twice on the first turn. |
| **Ward** | Cannot be targeted by the opponent's spells or modifier effects for 1 turn after deployment. Does NOT protect against combat damage, AoE events, or untargeted effects. |

### Notable Keyword Combinations

- **Deathtouch + Piercing** — Kills any blocker with 1 damage; all remaining ATK pierces through to face
- **Flying + Deathtouch** — Evasive; kills anything it touches; requires Flying or Reach to block
- **Taunt + Deathtouch** — Forces attackers in; guarantees favorable trades
- **Shield + Taunt** — Soaks a hit AND forces engagement — cornerstone of defensive Order builds
- **Haste + Deathtouch** — Deploy and immediately kill an enemy creature
- **Ward + Taunt** — Cannot be removed by targeted spells before combat, AND forces engagement

## Part 7: The Evolution System

### The Path

**Common → Uncommon → Rare → Epic → Legendary**

Chaos mote cost never changes through evolution.

### How Cards Earn Evolution Energy

All creatures and Planar Ruins in your deck accumulate chaos energy simultaneously, even if not drawn. Stabilizers and spells earn no evolution energy.

| Game Result | Energy Earned per Card |
|---|---|
| Win | 2 energy |
| Loss | 1 energy |

| Evolution Step | Energy Required | ~Games at 50% Winrate |
|---|---|---|
| Common → Uncommon | 15 | ~10 games |
| Uncommon → Rare | 30 | ~20 more |
| Rare → Epic | 50 | ~33 more |
| Epic → Legendary | 75 | ~50 more |
| **Total (Common → Legendary)** | **170** | **~113 games** |

### The Planar Shard

When a card has enough energy, you also need a **Planar Shard** of the appropriate tier. Shards are purchased with Chaos Dust.

| Shard | Cost |
|---|---|
| Uncommon Shard | 30 Dust |
| Rare Shard | 60 Dust |
| Epic Shard | 120 Dust |
| Legendary Shard | 240 Dust |

### Choosing Order or Chaos

When you evolve, you choose how to channel the energy:

- **Channel toward Order** — 70% chance of Order evolution, 30% chance of Chaos
- **Channel toward Chaos** — 70% chance of Chaos evolution, 30% chance of Order

The 30% surprise isn't a failure — it's a different outcome that might suit a different build.

### What Each Evolution Grants

1. **Higher base stats** — ATK and HP increase
2. **A triggered ability** — tied to Order or Chaos events
3. **An effect modifier** — drawn from the modifier pool
4. **A new AI-generated name** — you pick from 2–3 candidates
5. **New AI-generated art** — built on the previous art; your Legendary carries visual DNA from every stage
6. **New AI-generated flavor text** — the card's lore evolves with it

### Instability Changes Through Evolution

| Evolution Step | Chaos Outcome | Order Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Common → Uncommon | +1 instability | No change |
| Uncommon → Rare | +1 instability | No change |
| Rare → Epic | +1 instability | -1 instability |
| Epic → Legendary | +1 instability | -2 instability |

A card through four Order evolutions can end up significantly more stable. A card through four Chaos evolutions can reach instability 8 from a base of 2. No card is ever locked into an archetype by its base instability.

### Evolution Produces Unique Cards

Two players who both start with the same base card and evolve it four times will end up with dramatically different Legendaries — different modifiers, triggers, instability values, and art that diverged at each step. Every Legendary has a personal history that no other player can replicate.

## Part 8: Modifiers

Modifiers are permanent additions earned one per evolution step. A Common has zero. A fully evolved Legendary has four.

### How Modifier Attunement Works

Every modifier has two parts:

**Base Effect** — Always active. The modifier's floor.

**Attuned Bonus** — Only active when the matching event type fired on your most recent chaos roll. Attunement state persists until your next chaos roll.

**Example — Chaos-attuned:**
> *Chaos-Forged Blade*
> Base: +1 ATK
> Chaos Attunement: +2 ATK instead when your last roll triggered Chaos

**Example — Order-attuned:**
> *Warden's Resilience*
> Base: +1 HP
> Order Attunement: This creature also has Shield when your last roll triggered Order

**Example — Extra-powerful with penalty:**
> *Entropic Surge*
> Base: +2 ATK
> Chaos Attunement: +4 ATK instead
> Order Penalty: -1 ATK (net +1 ATK total)

### Universal vs. Faction Modifiers

**Universal modifiers** are available to all factions. **Faction modifiers** are exclusive and must reference the faction's exclusive mechanic (Augment, Bond, Corruption, Exalt, or Persist). At every evolution, you see at least one of each.

### Modifier Selection and Subscription Tiers

| Tier | Universal Options | Faction Options | Total Choices |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | 1 | 1 | Pick 1 of 2 |
| Mid tier | 1 | 2 | Pick 1 of 3 |
| High tier | 2 | 2 | Pick 1 of 4 |

All tiers draw from the same pools at the same power level. The advantage of higher tiers is build sculpting precision — not power.

## Part 9: Triggered Abilities

Triggered abilities fire as a one-time event when their specific condition is met. A card can have up to four — one per evolution step.

| Trigger | Fires When |
|---|---|
| **ON_ORDER** | Your chaos roll produces an Order event |
| **ON_CHAOS** | Your chaos roll produces a Chaos event |
| **ON_PLAY** | The creature enters the battlefield |
| **ON_DEATH** | The creature is destroyed (primary Persist trigger) |
| **ON_DAMAGE_TAKEN** | The creature takes damage (not if Shield absorbed) |
| **ON_ATTACK** | The creature is declared as an attacker |
| **ON_BLOCK** | The creature is assigned as a blocker |

Ability power scales with evolution tier:

| Tier | Power Level | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Uncommon | Minor | ON_ORDER: this creature gets +1 ATK this turn |
| Rare | Moderate | ON_CHAOS: this creature gets +2 ATK this turn |
| Epic | Significant | ON_ORDER: draw 1 card |
| Legendary | Game-impacting | ON_CHAOS: deal 2 damage to all enemy creatures |

## Part 10: Planar Ruins

### How Ruins Work

- **Take a creature slot** — a Ruin means one fewer creature in your lineup
- **High HP, zero ATK** — HP = mana cost × 3 + 1 (a 4-cost Ruin has 13 HP)
- **Passive benefit** while on the field (healing ticks, ATK bonuses, mana generation, card selection, etc.)
- **Opponents can attack your Ruins** with creatures
- **Destruction penalty** fires on your side for one turn when a Ruin is destroyed
- **Maximum 1 on the field** at a time; **maximum 2 in deck**

Ruins cannot be purchased with Chaos Dust — they must be earned through matches, quests, and season milestones.

### Ruin Evolution

All Ruins start as **neutral** — playable in any deck. After 10 battles with the Ruin in your deck, it can be evolved **once** into a **faction-specific** version, locked to that faction. Evolved Ruins have stronger, faction-complementary effects and faction-themed destruction penalties.

### The Eight Neutral Ruins at Launch

|---|---|---|

| The Anchor Plinth | 3 | HP buff to your creatures |
| The Mote Well | 3 | Mana generation |
| The Sight Glass | 4 | Card selection |
| The War Cairn | 4 | ATK buff to your creatures |
| The Threshold Gate | 5 | Evolution energy bonus |
| The Communion Altar | 5 | Group healing |

## Part 11: Stabilizers and Manipulation

### Stabilizers — The Stability Zone

Stabilizers go into the **stability zone** — a dedicated area separate from your 5 creature board slots. They are **free to play** (cost 0 chaos motes), with a limit of **one stabilizer per turn** from hand. Once in the stability zone, each stabilizer has an **activated ability**: during your main phase, tap a ready stabilizer to trigger its effect immediately. After activating, that stabilizer goes **on cooldown** and cannot be activated again until your next turn. Multiple stabilizers can be activated in one turn if each is individually off cooldown.

Stabilizers cannot be targeted or damaged by creature attacks. They do not contribute to instability passively — only when activated.

| Card | Cost | Activated Effect | Cooldown |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chaos Anchor** | FREE | Activate → -1 instability per creature currently on your board (immediate) | Until your next turn |
| **Warding Pillar** | FREE | Activate → your avatar's instability modifier is doubled until your next turn | Until your next turn |
| **Chaos Rift** | FREE | Activate → +1 instability per creature currently on your board (immediate) | Until your next turn |
| **Entropy Engine** | FREE | Activate → if your last chaos roll was a Chaos event, your highest-ATK creature gets +1 ATK permanently | Until your next turn |
| **Void Lens** | FREE | Activate BEFORE your chaos roll → your next roll counts as Order or Chaos (your choice). Cannot override "Nothing" results. Must be activated before the roll. | Until your next turn |

### Manipulation Spells

These are **spells** (not stabilizers) — they cost mana, resolve once, and go to the graveyard.

| Card | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Binding Ward** | 2 | Set your instability to 5 for this turn only |
| **Entropy Spike** | 2 | Set your instability to 15 for this turn only |

**Strategic notes:**
- **Void Lens** is the most powerful stabilizer — guaranteeing your event type is extremely strong. Remember: it must be activated BEFORE the dice roll, not after
- **Warding Pillar** pairs with strongly Order-leaning avatars (Serevain's -6 doubled to -12 = near-certain Order events this turn)
- **Chaos Anchor** is your safety valve if your Chaos deck is running too hot — activate when you have a full board for maximum instability reduction
- **Entropy Engine** rewards sustained Chaos play with permanent snowballing ATK — activate right after a Chaos roll fires
- Because stabilizers are free and don't cost board slots, every deck can afford to include several without sacrificing the creature curve

### Chaos Dust Sources

| Source | Chaos Dust |
|---|---|
| Win a match | 15 Dust |
| Lose a match | 5 Dust |
| Daily quest (3 per day) | 25–50 Dust each |
| Weekly quest (2 per week) | 100–200 Dust each |
| Season milestone | 50–500 Dust (tiered) |

### Spending Chaos Dust

| Purchase | Cost |
|---|---|
| Card Pack (own faction, 3 random Commons) | 100 Dust |
| Card Pack (other faction + unlock) | 150 Dust |
| Specific Common | 50 Dust |
| Uncommon Planar Shard | 30 Dust |
| Rare Planar Shard | 60 Dust |
| Epic Planar Shard | 120 Dust |
| Legendary Planar Shard | 240 Dust |
| Avatar unlock | 300 Dust |

Duplicate protection prevents packs from giving you a third+ copy of a Common you already own. Additional factions are unlocked for 150 Dust (one card pack from that faction).

### Four Progression Tracks

Every game advances all four tracks simultaneously:

1. **Player Level** — XP → unlocks game modes and cosmetic options
2. **Faction Mastery** — XP from games with your faction → unlocks faction-specific cosmetics and avatars
3. **Card Veterancy** — Chaos energy accumulates toward evolution for all creatures and Planar Ruins in your deck (stabilizers and spells earn no energy)
4. **Season Rank** — Competitive ladder → Chaos Dust rewards and exclusive card frames

### Onboarding: Starting Your Collection

1. Receive trial decks for all five factions — 30-card premade decks (Commons, with stabilizers and 1 Ruin)
2. Play 1–3 matches with each to feel out the factions
3. Commit to one faction — that trial deck becomes your real collection (fully evolvable)
4. Start with 30 owned cards in your faction, a starter avatar, enough shards to evolve 2–3 cards, and starter Dust

## Part 13: Subscriptions

**Free players have full mechanical access.** You can build competitive decks, reach high ladder ranks, and evolve cards to Legendary without spending a dollar. Subscriptions expand the experience — they do not create power advantages.

| Tier | Price | Modifier Selection | Deck Slots | Monthly Bonus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Free** | $0 | Pick 1 of 2 | 3 | — |
| **Mid Tier** | ~$5–8/mo | Pick 1 of 3 | 6 | +3 Commons/mo, +50% quest Dust |
| **High Tier** | ~$10–15/mo | Pick 1 of 4 | 10 | +5 Commons/mo, +100% quest Dust, 1 free Legendary Shard/mo |

**What subscriptions do not do:**
- Give access to exclusive or stronger modifiers — all tiers draw from the same pool
- Restrict card evolution — free players evolve to Legendary just as far as subscribers
- Make free players lose to paying players — a skilled free player should always beat a paying player

**What subscriptions do:**
- Show more modifier options at each evolution, enabling more precise build sculpting
- Provide Refined and Prismatic Planar Shards for higher-resolution evolution art
- Increase card collection limits and deck slots
- Speed up shard acquisition through Dust bonuses and free monthly shards

## Quick Reference

### Turn Order

1. Start of Turn effects (Corruption damage, aura checks, Ruin effects)
2. Chaos Roll (D20 vs. your instability)
3. Event Resolution (Order or Chaos event fires, then triggered abilities)
4. Draw 1 card, gain 1 chaos mote
5. Main Phase (60-second timer)
6. Declare Attackers
7. Defending player Assigns Blockers (60-second timer)
8. Combat Resolution (simultaneous damage)
9. End of Turn — temporary effects expire, turn passes

### Instability Summary

- Roll **<** instability = Chaos event
- Roll **>** instability = Order event
- Roll **=** instability = nothing
- Clamped between 1 and 20
